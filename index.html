How the spacing check works

The model assumes 60-inch rounds, 6 × 2.5-ft banquet tables, and a 2-ft seated-chair allowance beyond each table edge. It aims for a 3-ft gap between occupied chair spaces; it highlights clashes and gaps under that target. Table-leg positions, pulled-out chairs, serving routes, door swings and local requirements need an on-site check. An automated check of these candidate placements is not proof that no other arrangement could fit.

Before choosing the final layout

What Milano needs to confirm

  1. Usable Monza wall lengths, including the restroom-wall recess; actual stage, doors and round-table diameters.
  2. A measured version of option B with all 119 seats, the dance floor, serving routes and both main entrance approaches.
  3. Head-table supports and corner seats; cake-table and boba-cart sizes; chairs pulled out for sitting and standing.
  4. Lobby access for the bar, trio, photo booth and queues; the trio’s power and sound requirements.

If the measured room is near the smaller test sizes, the tested layouts have conflicts. Ask Milano for another measured setup or additional contracted space; don’t solve it by squeezing in chairs or splitting unrelated guests across oversized tables.
